In UniProt, pan proteomes are generated by clustering proteins from multiple proteomes of the same species, then selecting one representative sequence per protein cluster. UniProt pan proteomes are generated at every UniProt [release](https://www.uniprot.org/help/synchronization).

# Workflow overview

## 1. Assessment of species and proteome eligibility
A species is considered for pan proteome generation when at least three eligible proteomes are available and the taxonomic assignment is at species level. 

Proteomes are considered ineligible if:

- The proteome is [excluded](https://www.uniprot.org/help/proteome_exclusion_reasons)
- The proteome contains a very low protein count (<180) or only contains non-nuclear components
- The proteome does not belong to a defined species (a proteome must have a species name with complete binomial nomenclature including both genus and epithet). Example: A defined species is *Salmonella enterica* (taxonomy ID: [28901](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/taxonomy/28901/)), as opposed to an undefined species such as *Salmonella sp.* (taxonomy ID: [599](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/taxonomy/599/)) where the taxonomy is not resolved at the species rank (e.g., genus-level assignment like "*Salmonella spp.*")
- The proteome is from a pathogen surveillance project, primarily identified through its corresponding BioProject ID listed in NCBI's [Pathogen Detection resource](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pathogen/Results/BioProject_Hierarchy/latest.bioproject_hierarchy.txt)
- The proteome is from an organism with the status ‘*Candidatus*’

## 2. MMseqs2 protein clustering is performed for each species

Proteins from all eligible proteomes are clustered with MMseqs2 using parameters such as:

- Sequence identity. This sets the minimum similarity between two sequences for them to be considered a match. The sequences must be at least 90% identical
- Coverage. This sets the coverage of the alignment between two sequences for them to be considered a match. At least 50% of the sequence lengths must be covered by the alignment
- Bidirectional alignment coverage. The alignment coverage requirement is applied to both the query and target sequences

Full parameter details can be found in [README_pp_mmseqs](https://ftp.uniprot.org/pub/databases/uniprot/current_release/knowledgebase/pan_proteomes/README_pp_mmseqs).

## 3. Filtering for singleton protein clusters

For species with more than 10 proteomes, singleton protein clusters (clusters contributed to by only one proteome) are removed, except when these belong to [reference proteomes](https://www.uniprot.org/help/reference_proteome). For species with 10 or fewer proteomes, all protein clusters are retained.

## 4. Representative protein sequences are selected hierarchically

From each protein cluster, a representative sequence is chosen in the following order of priority: 

1. UniProtKB entry over UniParc-only entry
2. Entry from a reference proteome over a non-reference proteome
3. [Reviewed/Swiss-Prot](https://www.uniprot.org/help/uniprotkb_sections) entry over unreviewed/TrEMBL entry
4. [GeneCentric canonical sequence](https://www.uniprot.org/help/canonical_and_isoforms) over an isoform
5. Entry with sequence length closest to the mode length of the protein cluster
6. Finally, if a tiebreaker is needed, the entry from the earliest created proteome is selected

If entries from multiple reference proteomes are present in the same protein cluster, the representative is chosen according to the order of reference proteome preference:

1. Proteomes of special community interest identified by UniProtKB curators, and proteomes identified as ‘type strains’ (“assembly from type material” in [NCBI assembly metadata](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genomes/genbank/assembly_summary_genbank.txt) indicating the principal reference genome assembly for a species, when available)
2. Proteomes with the highest number of reviewed/Swiss-Prot entries
3. Proteomes with the highest [BUSCO completeness score](https://www.uniprot.org/help/assessing_proteomes)
4. If a tiebreaker is needed, then the earliest created reference proteome is selected

## 5. Generation of standardized output files

- FASTA (pp<taxid>.fasta.gz): One representative sequence per protein cluster, with Protein Frequency (PF) in header
- Matrix (pp<taxid>_matrix.tsv.gz): Cluster-by-proteome count matrix
- Stats (pp<taxid>_stats.json): Summary statistics for the species

## 6. Release and versioning

The species pan proteome selection workflow is run as part of the production pipeline during each UniProt release cycle. Updates are released with each [UniProt release](https://www.uniprot.org/help/synchronization); the corresponding release identifier is recorded in the stats files ((pp<taxid>_stats.json), and any methodology or format changes are tracked in [CHANGELOG](https://ftp.uniprot.org/pub/databases/uniprot/current_release/knowledgebase/pan_proteomes/CHANGELOG).

# FAQs

## 1. How can pan proteomes be used?

UniProt pan proteomes encompass all non-redundant proteomes and enable comparative proteome analyses. They can also support gene diversity studies, pathogen resistance development, and vaccine- and drug-target discovery.

## 2. What is a singleton protein cluster?

A singleton is a protein cluster which consists of one or more protein(s) from only one proteome.

## 3. Are singleton protein clusters always removed from pan proteomes? 

No. For species with >10 proteomes, singletons are removed unless these belong to reference proteomes. For species with ≤10 proteomes, all protein clusters are included.

## 4. Why does a species not have a pan proteome?  

Most commonly because fewer than three eligible proteomes are available, taxonomy is not at the species level, the taxon is assigned the status Candidatus, or available proteomes are excluded by eligibility/quality/surveillance criteria. Please see the section above ‘Assessment of species and proteome eligibility.’

## 5. What is Protein Frequency?

Protein frequency (PF) is the percentage of clustered proteomes for a species that contribute at least one sequence to a cluster (rounded up to the nearest integer). PF=100% means the cluster is shared across all clustered proteomes.

## 6. What are “core” and “accessory” proteins?  

Proteins present in almost all proteomes analyzed are termed ‘core proteins’, and they have high PF values (conventionally defined as >90%). Proteins present in a subset of the proteomes analyzed (conventionally defined between 10-90%) are termed ‘accessory proteins’ and have lower PF values. We do not give strict definitions of core and accessory protein clusters. The PF value is provided so users can filter according to their use case. We also provide a script [`filter_pp_fasta.py`](https://ftp.uniprot.org/pub/databases/uniprot/current_release/knowledgebase/pan_proteomes/filter_pp_fasta.py)that can filter a pan proteome FASTA file by the user-specified PF.

## 7. How is the representative sequence chosen for each protein cluster?  

The representative sequence is identified using a fixed priority hierarchy (UniProtKB vs UniParc, reference proteome status, review status, canonical status, creation date tiebreakers), which ensures consistent and reproducible representation. Please see the section above “Representative protein sequences are selected hierarchically”

## 8. How can I find the corresponding genome assembly for a protein in a pan proteome? 

You can use the proteome identifier (UPID) associated with that entry (shown in the pan proteome [FASTA header](https://www.uniprot.org/help/fasta-headers) and matrix columns (see FAQ below)) to access its proteome page. The corresponding genome assembly is listed in the “Genome assembly and annotation” section in the summary information at the top of the proteome page.

Example: In [UP000181909](https://www.uniprot.org/proteomes/UP000181909) the genome assembly is GCA_900119365.1 from ENA/EMBL.

## 9. Can I identify exact protein sequences behind matrix counts?  

The matrix allows you to identify the representative sequence selected for each protein cluster, but not each individual protein sequence contributing to the count. 

In the matrix file:

- Rows represent a protein cluster identified by [UniParc IDs](https://www.uniprot.org/help/uniparc_id) (UPI)
- Columns represent proteomes, identified by [proteome IDs](https://www.uniprot.org/help/proteome_id) (UPID)
- Each cell indicates the number of proteins from a given proteome that belong to the protein cluster

For example:

| cluster         | proteomes_count  |UP000123XXX   |UP000456XXX   |UP000789XXX   |
|:----------------|:-----------------|:-------------|:-------------|:-------------|
| UPI00123XXXXX   | 3                | 1            | 1            | 1            |
| ...             |                  |              |              |              |
| UPI00789XXXXX   | 1                |0             |2             |0             |


Where:

- UPI00123XXXXX is the representative protein sequence chosen for the cluster. The cluster contains proteins from 3 different proteomes (proteomes_count=3), where each of the 3 proteomes (UP000123XXX, UP000456XXX, UP000789XXX) contributes 1 protein to the cluster
- Similarly, UPI00789XXXXX is the representative protein sequence for another cluster. This cluster has 2 proteins coming from only 1 proteome (UP000456XXX, proteomes_count=1) indicating paralogs

A value of 0 means that the proteome does not contain a protein assigned to that cluster.
Note: proteomes_count refers to the number of proteomes represented within  the cluster, not the total number of proteins in the cluster.

To retrieve the data:

- View the protein sequence via its UniParc entry. Example: [UPI000DF01F15](https://www.uniprot.org/uniparc/UPI000DF01F15/entry)
- View the corresponding proteome. Example: [UP000181909](https://www.uniprot.org/proteomes/UP000181909)

MMseq2 clustering parameters can be found in the [README_pp_mmseqs](https://ftp.uniprot.org/pub/databases/uniprot/current_release/knowledgebase/pan_proteomes/README_pp_mmseqs), This document includes the MMseqs2 parameter set, and the MMseqs2 version used.

## 11. Where should users go for full technical details of the pan proteomes selection workflow?

Users can access the full technical details of the pan proteome selection workflow in the Pan Proteomes [README and README_pp_* files](https://ftp.uniprot.org/pub/databases/uniprot/current_release/knowledgebase/pan_proteomes/) and the [associated publication](https://ftp.uniprot.org/pub/databases/uniprot/current_release/knowledgebase/pan_proteomes/CITATION) (in preparation).

## 12. What should users consider when interpreting pan proteome results?

Pan proteome results depend on the quality and completeness of the underlying assemblies and annotations, and the clustering algorithm used ie. Mmseqs2. It should be noted that cluster representatives reflect a single sequence and not necessarily the full diversity of all cluster members.

Potential limitations may include:

 - Missing, fragmented, or spurious proteins in source genome databases
 - Variability in annotation quality and methods between genomes
 - Contamination in genome assemblies
 - Clustering limitations arising from sequence-similarity-based grouping, which may merge distinct proteins or split homologous proteins (or protein isoforms) into separate clusters
 - The number and diversity of proteomes analysed can affect the distribution of core, and accessory proteins
