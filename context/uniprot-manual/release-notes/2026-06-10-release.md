# Headline

## To drink, or not to drink?

Your mouth is dry? You may be thirsty. It has long been thought that the sensation of dry mouth and throat was the primary manifestation of thirst, but it is much more complex. Thirst is triggered when there is a rise in plasma osmolality or a reduction in plasma volume or pressure. It is the [brain that responds to deficits in water homeostasis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27991901,25624099,29489747,29268095) and induces drinking behaviour, effectively bringing the blood metrics back to their normal physiological level. More precisely it is the neural circuits in the [lamina terminalis (LT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamina_terminalis), that underlie thirst perception and drinking behaviour. There are three nuclei in the LT, the subfornical organ (SFO), the organic vasculosum lamina terminalis (OVLT) and the median preoptic nucleus, all of which are anatomically interconnected. Interestingly, SFO and OVLT are located outside the blood-brain barrier and therefore have direct access to the circulation for plasma osmolarity sensing. However, the identity of the molecular 'thirst sensor(s)' remained elusive until recently.

Several months ago, the mystery was finally unveiled in [two publications](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38172113,40107268). The neuronal osmosensor is the mechanosensitive cation channel [TMEM63B](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/?query=gene:tmem63b+and+reviewed:true). TMEM63B is expressed in the majority of the SFO excitatory neurons. While TMEM63B-deficient neurons are healthy and have a normal response to non-osmolarity stimuli, they do not respond to hyperosmolarity. On the other hand, human TMEM63B can generate hyperosmolarity-induced currents, when transfected into completely unrelated cells, such as Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells. Under normal conditions, TMEM63B knockout mice behave similarly to wild-type littermates, but when tested in either an osmotic thirst model or dehydrated thirst model, they drink significantly less. It should be noted, however, that the water intake in knockout animals under both stress conditions is still larger than that in water-sated control mice. This suggests that TMEM63B is not the only actor in this process and that alternative pathways must also be involved. Hence although a major step has been taken, we may still expect some exciting findings in the field of thirst perception.

These two reports are not the first about TMEM63B acting as an osmosensor. In 2020, TMEM63B was found to be an [osmosensor required for survival of cochlear outer hair cells upon hearing onset](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32375046/). In this context, it was activated by hypotonic stress, and its deficiency caused hearing loss. The involvement of other [TMEM63 family members](https://www.tcdb.org/search/result.php?tc=1.A.17) in the perception of the environment and the body's needs has also been documented in very different experimental settings. For instance, in Drosophila, the TMEM63 channel detects [environmental humidity](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35780140/) and is involved in [food texture preference](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33657409/).

While waiting for some new discoveries about TMEM63 family members, why not have a drink and look up the recently updated [UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot TMEM63B entries](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/?query=gene:tmem63b+and+reviewed:true)

_Dear readers, after 23 years, this is my last headline. I hope that you enjoyed reading them as much as I enjoyed sharing my enthusiasm about proteins and the sorts of tricks life has invented to go on. Anne Estreicher_

# UniProt news

## Completing the reorganization of the protein space within UniProt 

Since release 2025_04 (15th October 2025) to 2026_02 (10th June 2026), UniProt has [undergone major changes](https://www.uniprot.org/help/refprot_only_changes) which are now public:

**UniProt Knowledgebase** ([UniProtKB](https://www.uniprot.org/help/uniprotkb)):

- The UniProtKB is now comprised of entries from reference proteomes, and additional selected entries with experimental or biologically important data
- The number of UniProtKB accessions has been reduced by 43%
- [ProtNLM2](https://www.uniprot.org/help/ProtNLM) annotation of approximately 26,000 unreviewed/TrEMBL entries available for [download](https://ftp.ebi.ac.uk/pub/contrib/UniProt/ProtNLM2/List_of_UniProt_accessions_that_have_ProtNLM2_annotations.tsv)
 
**[Proteomes](https://www.uniprot.org/help/proteome):**

- New [reference proteome](https://www.uniprot.org/help/reference_proteome) selection [workflow](https://www.uniprot.org/help/ref_proteomes_workflow) (publication in preprint ['A novel method to select Reference Proteomes in UniProt'](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.12.720148v1)) to improve the representation of species biodiversity in the UniProtKB
- New [pan proteome workflow](https://www.uniprot.org/help/pan_proteomes_workflow) to generate [pan proteomes](https://www.uniprot.org/help/pan_proteomes)  for [download](https://ftp.uniprot.org/pub/databases/uniprot/current_release/knowledgebase/pan_proteomes) for all species with at least 3 proteomes
- New [proteome similarity](https://www.uniprot.org/help/proteome_similarity) information
- Updates to the [proteome exclusion reasons](https://www.uniprot.org/help/proteome_exclusion_reasons)
- Increased the number of species with a reference proteome by 34%
- Increased the number of reference proteomes by 36%
- Proteome status category changes: ['Redundant proteomes'](https://www.uniprot.org/help/redundancy) deprecated; 'Other proteome' renamed to 'Non-reference proteome'
- Major updates to the [proteomes help documentation](https://www.uniprot.org/help?query=proteomes) in line with the changes described above

**[UniParc](https://www.uniprot.org/help/uniparc):**

- New [UniParc sequence annotation pages](https://www.uniprot.org/help/uniparc_sequence_annotation_pages) - a dedicated annotation page as an alternative for the deleted TrEMBL entries and for active entries from external source databases
- New automatic annotations generated by [UniRule](https://www.uniprot.org/help/unirule) and [ARBA](https://www.uniprot.org/help/arba) rules are added to the [sequence annotation pages](https://www.uniprot.org/help/uniparc_sequence_annotation_pages)
 
**[UniRef](https://www.uniprot.org/help/uniref):**

- Optimization of UniParc sequences used for clustering in UniRef to support the reorganization of the UniProtKB protein space
- Prioritization of higher-quality RefSeq entries, selected by rule-based predictions, annotation score, and proteome quality
- Maintenance of UniRef90 and UniRef50 cluster coverage despite a reduction in the overall number of clusters:
    - UniRef100: reduced by 52% (221 million entries)
    - UniRef90: reduced by 36% (121 million entries)
    - UniRef50: reduced by 36% (39 million entries)

# UniParc news

## UniParc XML changes
We have replaced the ```proteome_id``` and ```component``` property types by the new property type ```proteomeid_component```. Its value is a concatenation of the proteome identifier and the component name, joined by a colon. This change was necessary to enable the representation of more than one proteome/component pair for a given ```dbReference``` element, e.g.
```
<dbReference type="UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot" id="Q9ZC49" version_i="1" active="Y" version="1" created="2001-01-24" last="2026-06-10">
  <property type="gene_name" value="glsA2"/>
  <property type="protein_name" value="Glutaminase 2"/>
  <property type="NCBI_GI" value="12585247"/>
  <property type="proteomeid_component" value="UP000000815:Chromosome"/>
  <property type="proteomeid_component" value="UP000001019:Chromosome"/>
  <property type="NCBI_taxonomy_id" value="632"/>
</dbReference>
```

## UniParc API changes

In the UniParc database endpoint, the two existing fields ```proteomeId``` and ```component``` have been replaced with a list of ```proteomes```, where each proteome has the fields ```id``` and ```component```, e.g
```
{
  "proteomes": [
    {
      "id": "proteome1",
      "component": "component1"
    },
    {
      "id": "proteome2",
      "component": "component2"
    }
  ]
}
```

API queries must be done with the fully qualified proteome/component name in the form of ```<id>:<component>```.


## UniParc website search changes

The advanced search form for UniParc has been adapted with a new ```Proteome Component``` search field, e.g. ```proteomecomponent:"UP000000815:Chromosome"```.


# UniProtKB news

## Expanded human proteome

The human proteome ([UP000005640](https://www.uniprot.org/proteomes/UP000005640)) was expanded with around [121,000 new protein-coding transcripts](https://www.ensembl.info/2025/09/02/ensembl-115-has-been-released/) that have been added to the GRCh38 human reference gene set based on long-read RNA-seq data using the TAGENE pipeline.

## Cross-references to AbasyAtlas

Cross-references have been added to the AbasyAtlas database, a comprehensive atlas of regulatory networks, functional modules, system-level elements, and global structural properties.

AbasyAtlas is available at [https://abasy.ccg.unam.mx/](https://abasy.ccg.unam.mx/).

The format of the explicit links is:

| Field | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Resource abbreviation | AbasyAtlas |
| Resource identifier | UniProtKB accession number |

Example: [P0A6D0](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P0A6D0)

[Show all entries having a cross-reference to AbasyAtlas.](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/?query=database:abasyatlas)


### Text format

Example: [P0A6D0](https://rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P0A6D0.txt)

```
DR   AbasyAtlas; P0A6D0; -.
```

### XML format

Example: [P0A6D0](https://rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P0A6D0.xml)

```
<dbReference type="AbasyAtlas" id="P0A6D0"/>
```

### RDF format

Example: [P0A6D0](https://rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P0A6D0.ttl)

```
uniprot:P0A6D0
  rdfs:seeAlso <http://purl.uniprot.org/abasyatlas/P0A6D0> .
<http://purl.uniprot.org/abasyatlas/P0A6D0>
  rdf:type up:Resource ;
  up:database <http://purl.uniprot.org/database/AbasyAtlas> .
```

## Cross-references to NDEx

Cross-references have been added to NDEx, the Network Data Exchange resource for networks. These mappings include selected curated pathways from IQuery and cell-type-specific interaction maps from [MuSIC1 (HEK293) and Cell Maps for Artificial Intelligence (CM4AI) MuSIC2 (U2OS)](https://musicmaps.ai).

NDEx is available at [https://www.ndexbio.org](https://www.ndexbio.org).

The format of the explicit links is:

| Field | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Resource abbreviation | NDEx |
| Resource identifier | Resource identifier |
| Optional information 1 | Resource Comment |

Example: [A8MW95](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/A8MW95)

[Show all entries having a cross-reference to NDEx.](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/?query=database:ndex)


### Text format

```

```

### XML format

```
<dbReference type="NDEx" id="IQUERY-CP-BECN2">
  <property type="comment" value="2 NDEx IQuery Curated Pathways"/>

```
